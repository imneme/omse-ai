# Readings of the probe photos from the test machine (core 3.02.02), Fri 06 Nov 2026

*Hart's-tongue Fern, reading the photo against `NOTES.md`'s expected screens. Written one probe at a time, close to my fold. The photos are in the photo (SHA256SUMS there).*

## P-1 identity and state: agrees with the VHDL; NextZXOS's state is now known

- **Identity, all as predicted:** `00=0A 01=32 0E=02` (Next, core 3.02.02). `0F=02`: **an issue 4 board** (Artix 7, one 2 MB SRAM chip). `02=02`: the last reset reads "hard", which is the firmware's handover after power-on (zxnext.vhd:1691-1698).
- **Memory:** `5F:55/AA 60:55/AA DF:55/AA`, so **2 MB**, as issue 4 must be.
- **As predicted:** `03=33` (+3 timing and type), `07=00`, `09=00`, `0A=11` (the firmware's value exactly), `0B=01`, `10=04`, `11=00`. MMU `FF FF 0A 0B 04 05 00 01`, `8E=0B` (ROM 3), `8F=00`. `80 81 8A 8C = 00`, `86-89 = FF FF FF 8F` (the hard-reset values), `7F=FF`. `C4=81 C5=00 C6=00 22=00 64=00`.
- **What NextZXOS changes from the firmware's handover.** This is new knowledge for the core's fast boot and for anyone writing tests against a booted machine:
  - **NR 0x82-0x85 = FF FF FF 8F.** NextZXOS turns *every* port back on, including sprites, Layer 2, port FF, DFFD and 0x6B, and sets NR 0x85 bit 7, so a soft reset re-enables them. That answers D-17.
  - **NR 0x12 = NR 0x13 = 09**: NextZXOS puts Layer 2 (active and shadow) at 16K bank 9, not the reset values 8 and 11.
  - **NR 0xC0 = 0A: stackless NMI is on** (bit 3), in IM 1 (bits 2:1 = 01), pulse mode. The core's NMI path needs §6.5's stackless mode for NextZXOS.
  - **NR 0xB8-0xBB = 82 00 00 F2**: NextZXOS's own divMMC entry points. RST 08 and RST 38 are on but 0x0000 isn't. In NR 0xBB: 3Dxx on, the 1FF8 area-off on, the tape traps 056A and 04D7 on, 0562 and 04C6 off, 0066 instant.
  - **NR 0x08 = DE: contention is disabled** (bit 6), with the speaker, DACs, Timex readback and turbosound on. That's NextZXOS's or the owner's setting. P-7 clears bit 6 for its run.
  - **NR 0x05 = 5B and NR 0x06 = A8**: the test machine's own joystick and NMI settings. Both sticks are MD pads on port 0x1F; the M1 multiface NMI is enabled and the DRIVE divMMC NMI isn't; AY mode is YM. Ratio's errata (NR 0x06 = 0xBD after the handover) concerns the default config; Melissa O'Neill's differs.

## P-8 Layer 2 over bank 5: the two copies confirmed; the 0x123B half spoiled by my probe

- **p8a:** the top third (Layer 2, rows 0-63) is dense multicoloured noise: stale SRAM, the "uninitialised" look main saw. There's no green and no red band.
- **p8b**, Layer 2 off: **the ULA's stripes, untouched**, across the whole top third.
- **What agrees with the VHDL (D-11's main claim).** The CPU's writes to bank 5 through the MMU reached the block-RAM copy, which the ULA shows (p8b's stripes). Layer 2 did **not** see them: no solid green, only another memory's stale contents. So **Layer 2 over bank 5 reads a different memory from the one the CPU and ULA share**, as zxnext.vhd:3020 and layer2.vhd:169-188 say. A core that keeps one bank 5 would show green.
- **What didn't work: the 0x123B-mapped writes.** The E3 fill and the red band reached neither copy: the noise stayed, and the ULA's stripes weren't disturbed. **Most likely my probe's fault.** Port 0x123B also holds a 3-bit bank *offset* (written with bit 4 set; zxnext.vhd:3862-3863). It is added to the CPU mapping's bank (zxnext.vhd:2926) but *not* to Layer 2's display, which uses NR 0x12 alone (layer2.vhd:172). Soft reset clears it, but NextZXOS can leave it set, and P-8 never wrote it. So the writes would have gone to SRAM bank 5 + offset.
- **To finish it:** P-8 should write `out (0x123B),0x10` (offset 0) before step 0. Reading the offset back isn't possible, since port 0x123B's read returns only the other fields (zxnext.vhd:3874).
- **Caution:** the misdirected 16K of E3 and 2K of E0 landed in SRAM bank 5 + offset, possibly NextZXOS's RAM. The soft reset followed and nothing seemed amiss, but a fixed P-8 must zero the offset first.
- **For the core:** keep the two copies (§3.11). Reset the 0x123B offset only on reset, and apply it to the CPU mapping only.

## P-7 INT against the beam: the real Next looks like 14335, not the core's 14337. Run the core to confirm

- **The photo:** a clean step, sixteen 0s then 1s from **k = 14** to k = 44, then **0 again from k = 45** to 63. That's a 31-sample pulse, the 32 T pulse at 1-T sampling, with no jagged edges. So the contended-read sync converged on the real machine (NR 03 was 33, restored after).
- **What the models predict** (corrected: I had written "about 25-56" in NOTES.md, forgetting that k = 0's IN starts one T-state before the frame ends). On omse-ai's 48K, the documented model with contention from 14335 T after INT:
  - the IN starts at frame T k−1 and samples about 11.5 T in;
  - INT is low from frame T 24 to 56;
  - so the first 1 is at **k = 14** and the first 0 after at **k = 46** (`check_p7.py` gives the IN positions).
- **The real Next:** 14 and 45. That matches the documented model to within one T-state on the falling edge. The extra half-T comes from NR 0x22's read path, registered on the 28 MHz clock and again on the CPU clock's falling edge (zxnext.vhd:5778-5788).
- **What the core should print if it puts contention 14337 T after INT:** INT sits 2 T earlier relative to the contention phase P-7 syncs to, so the first 1 comes at about **k = 12** and the first 0 at about 43.
- **So my reading is that the real Next behaves like 14335 in this test, not 14337**, and my earlier argument from the VHDL (14337) is off by 2 T in some convention, plausibly where the CPU first samples INT (top4:994-1003 against t80na). **The decider is still the core running P7.TAP.** If it prints 12/43, move INT 2 T later relative to contention, so that contention starts 14335 T after it; if it prints 14/45, INT stays. I'm sorry for having said "INT stays" before the photo; the rule was right and the photo answers it.

## P-6 wait states: the VHDL exactly

- **14 MHz:** A 16421, B 16422, C 16422 (VHDL 16420); D, E and F 14355 (VHDL 14354).
- **28 MHz:**

  | test | measured | VHDL |
  |---|---|---|
  | A NOP, SRAM | 10261 | 10262 |
  | B NOP, bank 7 | 8213 | 8214 |
  | C NOP, bank 5 | 10262 | 10262 |
  | D LD A,(HL) | 9226 | 9227 |
  | E LD (HL),A | 8203 | 8203 |
  | F LD A,(HL), bank 7 | 8203 | 8203 |

  Empty tests: 25 and 16 ticks.
- **Every figure is within 2 ticks** (32 cycles over runs of 130k-260k).
- **So, confirmed on her machine** (zxnext.vhd:3128-3140):
  - at 28 MHz, **one wait cycle on every read from SRAM or bank 5**, opcode fetches and stack pops included;
  - **no wait on writes** (E) and **none on bank-7 block-RAM reads** (B, F);
  - **no memory waits at 14 MHz**.
- The "no waits" alternative (8210 and 7177) is ruled out. The core and tests can build on this as fact.

## P-20 the zxnDMA: the VHDL exactly, the wiki wrong on every point it differed (read by Kelp Fern, Fri 06 Nov 2026 16:30 PST, from the photo, against NOTES.md)

Every line matches the figure dma.vhd predicts, in brackets on the screen:
- **SPI EB 02176 (02176).** The SPI wait holds the DMA's strobe up to state 15, where `spi_begin` fires again: two transfers and 34 clocks a byte, on the real machine. One transfer a byte would have been 01344, no SPI wait 00384. This is D-15 (Hart's-tongue's) seen through the DMA, so a DMA streaming port 0xEB gets every other card byte and sends each written byte twice, on 3.02.02.
- **P55 3.5 MHz 28544 (28544); 28 MHz 28209 (28208).** The prescaler's period is 32P t28 plus 3 CPU clocks, not the wiki's 875 kHz / P, the same at every speed (28160 for both). 28209 is within the stopwatch's 16-t28 grain (omse-ai gave 28207 on the other side).
- **P255 CB 04092 (04092), 0B 04092 (04092).** WR6 CB keeps the prescaler (the wiki says it resets it: 24), and the prescaler works through port 0x0B (Z80-DMA mode) too.
- **T11 T00 01299 01299.** Timing byte 11 is 4 clocks, like 00. And 01299 is a raw count, programming and the bus hand-over included, and it's omse-ai's 01299 exactly: the grant at T_Res and the release match the hardware to the tick.
- **STAT 1A 00 02 (1A 00 02).** A finished transfer's status reads 1A (IDLE clears T), not the wiki's 1B; counter 0200.
- **LEN0 01 (01).** A zxnDMA transfer of length 0 moves one byte (the wiki: 0).
- **LOCK 91 00 (00).** WR4 = 91h locks the register writes (R4_BYTE_2, no case); the soft reset at the key freed it.

So next-dma's DMA is confirmed on the test machine, and the second-sources DMA points (Rustyback's, Loam's, mine) are settled by the hardware.

## FIDDLEHEAD on the real Next: it sang as the emulator does (heard and looked at by Kelp Fern, Fri 06 Nov 2026 16:57 PST, from the photo, 6.4-8.6 s)

- **Pitch:** the first bell is 588.5 Hz on the Next against 587.2 Hz in omse-ai's film (designed as D5, 587.3), so 0.2% apart. That's near the measurement's grain; it could also be the Next's real master clock in its video mode, which the wiki says varies with HDMI/VGA timing.
- **Rhythm:** the note entries are 0.16 s apart, with 0.24 s before the chord, in both. The span from the second entry to the last is 0.880 s in both, equal to within the 5 ms analysis step. So the DMA's burst-mode rate (prescaler 55 plus the CPU's answer) matches the emulator's to within about half a percent.
- **Picture:** the frond grows the same at 0.3, 0.9, 1.5 and 2.3 s into the chime, and the curl closes as the last bells ring. At 1.5 s the real curl is one cell (about 27 ms) behind the film, inside the uncertainty of lining up the two clips (onset detection, 30 fps capture, the capture's audio/video offset).
- The sound at 5.3-5.6 s, before the chime, comes while the screen shows the loader's "Bytes:" lines. It isn't Fiddlehead's.
